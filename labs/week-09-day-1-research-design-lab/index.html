    Week 9 Day 1: Zoom Research Design Lab

    Research Design Sprint

    Today’s class is a 20-minute small-group Zoom lab. You will work in a group of 3 to design a small research study from beginning to end.

    By the end of the activity, your group should be able to explain:

    What do we want to know, who or what will we study, what will we measure or manipulate, and why is our chosen design appropriate?

    Step 1: Choose a Research Design

    Your group may choose one of the following:

    Option A: Survey Study

    Use a survey when you want to describe something, compare naturally occurring groups, or examine whether variables are related.

    Examples:

    • Is TikTok news use related to perceived news credibility?
    • How often do college students use generative AI for academic work?
    • Do people who follow sports journalists more frequently report greater knowledge of team news?

    Option B: Randomized Experiment

    Use an experiment when you want to test whether manipulating one factor causes a change in an outcome.

    Examples:

    • Does adding an AI-generated label to a news post affect perceived credibility?
    • Does a humorous advertisement produce greater purchase intention than a serious advertisement?
    • Does viewing a video apology rather than a written apology affect perceived sincerity?

    Remember: an RCT requires random assignment to conditions.

    Option C: Quasi-Experimental Study

    Use a quasi-experiment when you want to examine a possible effect but random assignment is not feasible.

    Examples:

    • Does student trust change before and after a university introduces a new AI-use policy?
    • Does a mental-health campaign appear to change attitudes on one campus compared with another campus that did not receive the campaign?
    • Does engagement with a news platform change after the platform introduces a new warning-label policy?

    Because participants are not randomly assigned, you must think carefully about alternative explanations.

    Your 20-Minute Challenge

    Choose a topic that interests your group. It can involve journalism, advertising, public relations, social media, sports communication, AI, health communication, entertainment, or another communication-related topic.

    If You Chose a Quasi-Experiment

    Choose a structure such as:

    • one-group pretest–posttest;
    • nonequivalent comparison group;
    • pretest–posttest with a comparison group; or
    • interrupted time series.

    Then explain:

    1. What event, policy, campaign, exposure, or intervention occurs?
    2. Who or what is measured?
    3. When is the outcome measured?
    4. Is there a comparison group?
    5. Why is random assignment not feasible?

    14–17 Minutes: Challenge Your Own Study

    Every design has limitations.

    Identify one major threat, weakness, or alternative explanation in your study.

    Examples might include:

    • convenience sampling;
    • poor measurement;
    • self-report bias;
    • selection bias;
    • history;
    • maturation or natural trends;
    • groups differing before the intervention;
    • something other than the treatment differing across experimental conditions.

    Our biggest concern:
    ....................................................................................
    ....................................................................................

    Now identify one realistic improvement.

    How we would improve the study:
    ....................................................................................
    ....................................................................................

    17–20 Minutes: Prepare Your 60-Second Explanation

    Your group should be ready to explain your design in approximately 60 seconds.

    Use this structure:

    We want to know whether/how ____________. We chose a ____________ design because ____________. We would study ____________. Our main predictor/independent variable is ____________, and our outcome is ____________. The main strength of this design is ____________, but one limitation is ____________.

    Group Roles

    With three people, use the following roles to keep the activity moving:

    Student 1 — Research Lead
    Keeps the group focused on the research question and makes sure the design actually answers it.

    Student 2 — Design Critic
    Looks for problems, alternative explanations, weak measurements, or mismatches between the question and the design.

    Student 3 — Recorder / Spokesperson
    Writes the group's final design and prepares the 60-second explanation.

    You should still make all major decisions together.

    You do not need to run the study or analyze data today. The purpose of this lab is to practice turning a research question into a coherent research design.
