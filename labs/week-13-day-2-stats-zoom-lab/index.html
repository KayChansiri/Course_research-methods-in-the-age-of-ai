    Week 13 Day 2 — Statistics Zoom Lab

    Today: Five groups. Five tests. One short research presentation per group. Each group has its own simulated communication dataset and a ready-to-run Google Colab notebook.

    During your 30-minute breakout:

    1. Open your assigned Colab notebook below. The dataset loads automatically; do not download a CSV.
    2. Run the code cells in order (▶). Double-click the YOUR ANSWER text cells and fill in the blanks.
    3. Explain your dataset: What does a row represent? What are your variables? Which are numeric or categorical? Check missing and repeated records.
    4. Explain the research question, run your test, and describe the plot and key result in plain language.
    5. Prepare a 3-minute group presentation: study → variables → plot → statistic + p-value → conclusion. Share your Colab screen.

    You are not expected to memorize or write Python code. Work together to understand what the output means. If p ≥ .05, say fail to reject H₀, not “accept H₀.”

    Your group notebook

    Group Topic / test Open notebook View dataset (optional)
    Group 1 Public relations — ANOVA Open Colab View CSV
    Group 2 Sports journalism — Regression Open Colab View CSV
    Group 3 Marketing / advertising — Correlation Open Colab View CSV
    Group 4 Strategic communication — t-test Open Colab View CSV
    Group 5 Sports journalism — Chi-square Open Colab View CSV

    Your research question

    Group 1 — ANOVA: Does average brand trust differ across three crisis-response styles?

    Group 2 — Regression: How does perceived source transparency predict sports story credibility?

    Group 3 — Correlation: Are perceived ad relevance and purchase intention related?

    Group 4 — t-test: Is average intention to share a public health message different between a facts message and a personal-story message?

    Group 5 — Chi-square: Is article clicking (Yes/No) associated with headline style (Neutral/Sensational)?

    Present to the class

    Keep it short. Show one figure or table, state your main result, and explain what the finding does and does not mean. Aim for 3 minutes per group.

    All five datasets are simulated for this teaching exercise.
