# Labs & Codebooks

This page brings together the guided activities and Google Colab notebooks used in JOUR 701. **Not every week includes a laboratory**. Many notebooks are simply resources for following along with demonstrations during regular in-person class meetings or for reviewing examples afterward.

You do not need to memorize Python code. Focus on understanding what each step accomplishes, why it is appropriate, and how to evaluate and interpret results. **Blackboard and the current course syllabus remain the official sources for meeting formats, schedules, and assignment instructions.**

## Week 14 — Computational Text Analysis & Sentiment Classification

### Day 1— Computational Text Analysis

In this lab, we will explore how computational methods can help researchers analyze written responses. Building on our Block 3 experimental dataset, we will practice text preprocessing, word frequency counts, and keyword analysis using Python.

**Google Colab Notebook:** [Open Week 14 day 1 Lab](https://colab.research.google.com/github/kaychansiri/research-methods-in-the-age-of-ai/blob/main/notebooks/Nov17_Computational_Text_Analysis_Colab.ipynb)

**What you will learn:**
- Prepare open-ended survey responses for computational analysis.
- Identify frequently occurring words and keywords.
- Interpret text patterns in the context of communication research.
- Understand how computational text analysis connects to research questions and measurement.

### Day 2  — Sentiment Classification

In this lab, we will learn how researchers classify written responses as positive, negative, or neutral using computational methods. We will compare human judgments with automated sentiment classification and connect the findings to statistical analyses from Block 3.

**Google Colab Notebook:** [Open week 14 day 2 Lab](https://colab.research.google.com/github/kaychansiri/research-methods-in-the-age-of-ai/blob/main/notebooks/Nov19_Sentiment_Classification_Colab.ipynb)

**What you will learn:**
- Understand sentiment classification and its applications in mass communication research.
- Compare rule-based sentiment analysis with a pretrained AI classifier.
- Evaluate AI-generated classifications against human judgment.
- Examine relationships between experimental message conditions and sentiment using statistical tests.

## Using These Materials

1. **Check Blackboard first** for the current meeting format, official Zoom link, readings, assignments, and any schedule changes.
2. **Open follow-along notebooks** during the relevant in-person lesson or afterward for review. A notebook link alone never means the class is online.
3. **For scheduled labs**, read the linked instructions and use the notebook assigned to your session or group.
4. **Run code in sequence and examine the output.** Prioritize methodological decisions and interpretation over memorizing syntax.
5. **Use only approved instructional or appropriately authorized data** in Colab and AI tools; do not upload confidential or personally identifiable information.
