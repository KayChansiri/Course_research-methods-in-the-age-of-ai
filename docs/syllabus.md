## Course Description

Research methods are changing as computation, large language models, and increasingly accessible analytical tools become part of everyday research.

In this course, you will learn how to move from a research question to evidence: reviewing literature, selecting appropriate research designs, organizing and analyzing data, interpreting findings, evaluating AI-assisted workflows, and communicating results.

The course combines traditional research methodology with modern research tools, including Python, Google Colab, pretrained language models, and generative AI. Programming is treated as a research tool rather than as an end in itself. No prior programming experience is required.

**Tools can assist research, but they cannot replace methodological judgment.**

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the course, you should be able to:

### Overall Research Competency

1. Explain the major stages of the research process and connect research questions to evidence, research design, data analysis, interpretation, and communication of findings.

### Block 1 — Modern Research Tools in the Age of AI

2. Explain foundational concepts behind contemporary AI and large language models at a level relevant to research.
3. Use Google Colab and basic Python to support reproducible research tasks.
4. Critically evaluate the capabilities, limitations, and appropriate uses of AI and pretrained language models in research workflows.

### Block 2 — Data Collection and Research Design

5. Develop research questions and hypotheses grounded in scholarly literature.
6. Distinguish among major quantitative research designs, including surveys, experiments, A/B tests, and quasi-experiments.
7. Identify and justify appropriate sampling, measurement, and data-collection strategies.

### Block 3 — Data Analysis and Interpretation

8. Select and conduct introductory statistical analyses, including correlation, t-tests, regression, ANOVA, and chi-square.
9. Apply introductory computational approaches to text data, including sentiment analysis or text classification.
10. Interpret analytical results in substantive rather than purely statistical terms, recognizing relevant assumptions and limitations.

### Block 4 — Responsible Research and Communication

11. Identify ethical, validity, and reproducibility concerns associated with computational and AI-assisted research.
12. Communicate research findings and their limitations to academic, industry, and other stakeholder audiences.

## Course Structure

The course is organized into four interconnected blocks that guide students from foundational computational literacy to research design, data analysis, and responsible communication of findings.

Because AI and computational tools are increasingly integrated into research practice, the course begins by introducing these tools and their capabilities and limitations. This foundation prepares students to evaluate when and how to use them throughout the research process.

### Block 1 — Modern Research Tools in the Age of AI

You will begin by examining how AI and computation are changing contemporary research. Topics include large language models, Transformer foundations, Google Colab, Python fundamentals, pretrained language models, and human evaluation of AI-assisted research workflows.

Through guided activities, you will compare human judgment with AI-assisted research tasks and learn to evaluate the capabilities, limitations, and appropriate uses of these tools.

The emphasis is on **computational literacy and critical evaluation**, not software engineering or memorizing programming syntax. This foundation prepares you to integrate computational tools thoughtfully into subsequent research activities.

### Block 2 — Data Collection and Research Design

Building on this foundation, you will move from understanding research tools to making methodological decisions.

Topics include research questions and hypotheses, sampling, measurement, surveys, randomized experiments, A/B testing, quasi-experimental designs, reliability, and validity.

You will learn to select and justify research designs based on the research question, recognizing that computational and AI tools can support—but cannot replace—sound methodological judgment.

### Block 3 — Data Analysis and Interpretation

You will apply your computational foundation and methodological knowledge to preparing, analyzing, and interpreting research data.

Topics include data cleaning, correlation, t-tests, linear regression, ANOVA, chi-square, and introductory computational text analysis and sentiment classification.

Most analytical topics follow a **concept → guided application** structure using Python and Google Colab.

The emphasis is on selecting appropriate analytical methods, interpreting findings, evaluating assumptions, and understanding limitations rather than extensive programming.

### Block 4 — Responsible Research and Communication

The final block brings together the knowledge and skills developed throughout the course.

You will integrate analytical reasoning with research ethics, responsible AI use, reproducibility, human oversight, research limitations, and stakeholder communication.

The course concludes with an applied group research project in which you use an instructor-provided dataset, select an appropriate analytical approach, interpret the results, and communicate what the evidence means.

## Technology

You will work with:

- **Google Colab** for browser-based computational notebooks
- **Python** for introductory data management and analysis
- **pandas and NumPy** for working with structured data
- **SciPy and statsmodels** for introductory statistical analysis
- **Matplotlib** for data visualization
- **Pretrained language models** for guided computational text activities
- **Generative AI** for selected research and coding activities
- **GitHub** for openly accessible instructional and computational materials

No prior programming experience is required.

You are not expected to memorize Python syntax or become an AI engineer. The emphasis is on understanding **what computational tools are doing, when they are appropriate, and how their outputs should be evaluated**.

## Course Readings

There is **no required textbook to purchase**.

Research methodology, computational tools, and AI are evolving quickly, and no single textbook adequately covers the full scope of the course.

Readings are therefore selected from freely available books, scholarly articles, technical documentation, and instructor-provided materials.

Assessments are aligned with the four instructional blocks.

| Final Stakeholder Research Presentation | Blocks 3–4: Analysis, Interpretation, and Communication | 30% |
| **Total** | | **100%** |

The assessment sequence moves from understanding and evaluating modern research tools, to making research-design decisions, to conducting and communicating an applied analysis.

Early assignments emphasize computational literacy and critical evaluation of AI-assisted research workflows.

The Research Design Brief focuses on developing a coherent study grounded in scholarly literature and sound methodological decisions.

The final project integrates data analysis, interpretation, responsible research practices, and communication of findings to stakeholders.

## AI and Human Oversight

AI is intentionally incorporated into selected course activities.

You will learn to use AI systems as **research tools whose outputs require evaluation**, rather than as substitutes for independent research judgment.

Depending on the activity, AI may support:

- Literature organization
- Classification
- Coding assistance
- Debugging
- Computational research workflows
- Methodological critique

The specific tools and permitted uses may vary by activity or assignment.

You remain responsible for:

- Verifying AI-generated information and code
- Understanding the analyses you conduct
- Identifying errors, biases, and limitations
- Protecting sensitive information
- Documenting AI use when required
- Making independent methodological and interpretive decisions

AI use must follow the instructions for each assignment. Using AI to generate work that is required to be completed independently is not permitted.

The emphasis throughout the course is on **human judgment, methodological reasoning, and responsible evaluation of computational tools**.
