# Quick Guide to Common Citation Styles

You may use any standard scholarly citation style for this assignment as long as you use the **same style consistently throughout the paper**.

Below are brief examples of several common citation styles you may encounter.

---

## APA Style (7th Edition)

APA is commonly used in the social sciences, communication, psychology, education, and related fields.

### In-text citation

**Parenthetical:**

> (Smith, 2024)

**Narrative:**

> Smith (2024) found that...

For two authors:

> (Smith & Lee, 2024)

For three or more authors:

> (Smith et al., 2024)

### Reference list

**Journal article:**

> Smith, J. A., & Lee, R. T. (2024). Trust in artificial intelligence systems. *Journal of Human-AI Research, 12*(3), 145–160. https://doi.org/10.xxxx/xxxxx

---

## MLA Style (9th Edition)

MLA is commonly used in the humanities, especially literature, language, and cultural studies.

### In-text citation

> (Smith 145)

The number usually refers to the page number.

### Works Cited

**Journal article:**

> Smith, Jane A., and Robert T. Lee. “Trust in Artificial Intelligence Systems.” *Journal of Human-AI Research*, vol. 12, no. 3, 2024, pp. 145–160. https://doi.org/10.xxxx/xxxxx.

---

## Chicago Style

Chicago style is common in history and some humanities and social science fields. It has two major systems: **Author-Date** and **Notes and Bibliography**.

### Chicago Author-Date

**In-text citation:**

> (Smith and Lee 2024, 145)

**Reference list:**

> Smith, Jane A., and Robert T. Lee. 2024. “Trust in Artificial Intelligence Systems.” *Journal of Human-AI Research* 12 (3): 145–160. https://doi.org/10.xxxx/xxxxx.

### Chicago Notes and Bibliography

Instead of parenthetical citations, this system commonly uses footnotes or endnotes.

**Footnote example:**

> 1. Jane A. Smith and Robert T. Lee, “Trust in Artificial Intelligence Systems,” *Journal of Human-AI Research* 12, no. 3 (2024): 145.

**Bibliography:**

> Smith, Jane A., and Robert T. Lee. “Trust in Artificial Intelligence Systems.” *Journal of Human-AI Research* 12, no. 3 (2024): 145–160. https://doi.org/10.xxxx/xxxxx.

---

## IEEE Style

IEEE is common in engineering, computer science, and technical fields.

### In-text citation

Sources are numbered in the order in which they first appear.

> Recent work has examined trust in AI systems [1].

### Reference list

**Journal article:**

> [1] J. A. Smith and R. T. Lee, “Trust in artificial intelligence systems,” *Journal of Human-AI Research*, vol. 12, no. 3, pp. 145–160, 2024.

---

## General Tips

- Pick **one citation style** and use it consistently.
- Every source cited in the text should appear in the reference list or bibliography.
- Every source in the reference list or bibliography should normally be cited in the paper.
- When using an established measurement scale, survey instrument, coding scheme, or experimental stimulus from prior research, cite the source of that measure.
- Include a DOI or stable URL when appropriate.
- Follow the capitalization, punctuation, author order, and italics rules of the style you choose.

The examples above are simplified. If you need more detail, consult the official or university-supported guide for the citation style you are using.
