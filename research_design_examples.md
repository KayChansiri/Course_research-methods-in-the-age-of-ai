# Research Design Examples

These examples are intended to show how the major components of a research proposal can fit together in a paper written in the style of a peer-reviewed journal article.

They are **illustrative examples**, not templates that must be copied exactly. Your own paper should be tailored to your research question, literature, variables, measures, and proposed design.

---

## Example 1: Survey Study

### Research Topic

**How is trust in artificial intelligence associated with people's willingness to use AI-generated health information?**

### 1. Introduction and Research Question

Generative AI systems are increasingly used to provide information and advice in health-related contexts. However, individuals may differ substantially in whether they are willing to rely on information generated by AI. One factor that may shape this willingness is trust in AI systems.

Prior research suggests that trust plays an important role in technology adoption and reliance. At the same time, excessive trust may lead users to accept inaccurate or inappropriate information without sufficient scrutiny. Understanding how trust relates to willingness to use AI-generated health information can therefore help researchers better understand when and why people choose to rely on AI systems.

**Research Question (RQ1):** How is trust in AI associated with willingness to use AI-generated health information?

**Hypothesis (H1):** Higher trust in AI will be associated with greater willingness to use AI-generated health information.

### 2. Brief Literature Review and Rationale

A literature review for this study would synthesize prior peer-reviewed research on topics such as:

- trust in automation or artificial intelligence,
- technology acceptance,
- reliance on algorithmic advice,
- use of AI for health information, and
- individual differences in attitudes toward AI.

Rather than summarizing each article separately, the review should explain what the studies collectively suggest, where findings are consistent or inconsistent, and what remains unknown.

For example:

> Prior research has shown that trust is an important predictor of whether individuals rely on automated systems (Author, Year; Author, Year). Studies of algorithmic decision-making further suggest that users' willingness to follow algorithmic recommendations varies depending on perceived competence, transparency, and prior experience (Author, Year). However, less is known about how general trust in AI relates to people's willingness to use AI-generated information in health contexts. The present study addresses this gap by examining the association between trust in AI and willingness to use AI-generated health information.

**Important:** In your own paper, replace the placeholder citations above with actual peer-reviewed sources and include full references.

### 3. Population and Sample

The population of interest could be adults in the United States who have experience using generative AI tools.

A proposed sample might include approximately **300 adults recruited through an online research panel**.

The paper should explain:

- who is eligible to participate,
- how participants will be recruited,
- the proposed sample size,
- the sampling strategy, and
- limitations of that sampling strategy.

### 4. Variables and Measurement

#### Trust in AI

Trust in AI would be measured using a multi-item scale adapted from an established measure in prior research.

Example items might include:

- "I generally trust AI systems to provide reliable information."
- "I feel confident relying on information generated by AI systems."
- "AI systems are generally dependable."

Participants could respond on a **1 (strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly agree)** scale.

The paper should cite the original or adapted scale and explain how responses would be combined into a measure of trust.

#### Willingness to Use AI-Generated Health Information

Willingness to use AI-generated health information could be measured using several items, such as:

- "I would consider using an AI system to help me understand health information."
- "I would be willing to consult an AI system when looking for general health information."
- "I would consider information from an AI system when making a health-related decision."

Responses could again use a **1–7 agreement scale**.

If these items are newly created rather than taken from an established scale, the researchers should explain and justify their development.

#### Additional Variables

Researchers might also measure potential control or explanatory variables such as:

- prior AI use,
- age,
- education,
- perceived AI literacy, or
- previous use of AI for health information.

### 5. Research Design and Procedure

This study would use a **cross-sectional online survey design**.

Participants would:

1. review the informed consent information,
2. answer questions about their prior experience with AI,
3. complete the trust-in-AI measure,
4. complete the willingness-to-use measure, and
5. provide relevant demographic information.

Researchers could then examine whether trust in AI is associated with willingness to use AI-generated health information.

Because the study is observational and cross-sectional, it could identify an **association**, but it would not establish that trust causes greater willingness to use AI.

### 6. Strengths and Limitations

Potential strengths include:

- efficient collection of data from a relatively large sample,
- ability to measure multiple psychological constructs, and
- use of established measures when available.

Potential limitations include:

- inability to make strong causal claims,
- reliance on self-reported intentions rather than actual behavior,
- possible sampling bias from an online panel, and
- common-method bias when predictor and outcome variables are measured in the same survey.

## Example 2: Experimental Study

### Research Topic

**Does the communication style of an AI assistant influence users' trust in its advice?**

### 1. Introduction and Hypothesis

AI assistants can communicate the same underlying recommendation in different ways. For example, an assistant may present its advice confidently or explicitly acknowledge uncertainty. These communication choices may influence how users evaluate and respond to the system.

Research on trust in automation and uncertainty communication suggests that expressions of confidence and uncertainty can shape perceptions of competence and reliability. However, the effect may depend on how uncertainty is communicated.

**Research Question (RQ1):** Does an AI assistant's communication style affect users' trust in its advice?

**Hypothesis (H1):** Participants exposed to an AI response that appropriately acknowledges uncertainty will report different levels of trust than participants exposed to the same recommendation expressed with high confidence.

### 2. Brief Literature Review and Rationale

A literature review for this study might synthesize research on:

- trust in AI and automation,
- uncertainty communication,
- confidence and advice taking,
- human-AI interaction, and
- perceptions of AI-generated recommendations.

For example:

> Research on human interaction with automated systems suggests that users rely not only on the content of a recommendation but also on cues about the system's confidence and reliability (Author, Year; Author, Year). Appropriate expressions of uncertainty may help calibrate user trust, whereas unjustified confidence may create inappropriate reliance (Author, Year). The present experiment therefore examines whether changing an AI assistant's communication style alters users' trust in otherwise similar advice.

Again, all claims in the actual assignment should be supported with appropriate peer-reviewed citations.

### 3. Population and Sample

The target population could be adult users of generative AI systems.

A proposed sample might consist of **200 participants recruited online**, with approximately 100 participants randomly assigned to each experimental condition.

The paper should explain eligibility criteria, recruitment, proposed sample size, and relevant sampling limitations.

### 4. Independent Variable and Experimental Conditions

The **independent variable** is the AI assistant's communication style.

Participants would be randomly assigned to one of two conditions.

#### Condition 1: High-Confidence Response

The AI gives a recommendation using confident language.

Example:

> "The best option is X. This approach is clearly the most appropriate choice."

#### Condition 2: Uncertainty-Acknowledging Response

The AI provides the same basic recommendation but appropriately communicates uncertainty.

Example:

> "Based on the information available, X appears to be the strongest option, although there is some uncertainty and other factors could affect the decision."

The substantive recommendation should remain as similar as possible across conditions so that the primary manipulated difference is **communication style**.

### 5. Dependent Variable and Measurement

The primary **dependent variable** would be trust in the AI assistant.

Trust could be measured with multiple survey items after participants view the response.

Example items:

- "I trust the recommendation provided by this AI assistant."
- "I consider this AI assistant reliable."
- "I would feel comfortable relying on this assistant's advice."

Participants might respond using a **1 (strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly agree)** scale.

The study might also measure secondary outcomes such as:

- perceived competence,
- perceived honesty,
- willingness to follow the recommendation, and
- perceived appropriateness of the response.

Whenever established measures are available, the paper should identify and cite them.

### 6. Research Design and Procedure

This study would use a **between-subjects randomized experiment**.

Participants would:

1. review informed consent information,
2. be randomly assigned to one of the two conditions,
3. read the same scenario,
4. view an AI-generated response corresponding to their assigned condition,
5. complete measures of trust and other outcomes, and
6. answer demographic and background questions.

Random assignment helps reduce systematic differences between the groups. Because the communication style is manipulated by the researchers, this design provides stronger evidence about whether communication style **causes** differences in trust than a cross-sectional survey would.

### 7. Strengths and Limitations

Potential strengths include:

- random assignment,
- greater ability to make causal inferences,
- controlled comparison of communication styles, and
- ability to isolate a specific feature of AI behavior.

Potential limitations include:

- an artificial study setting,
- reliance on a single scenario or AI response,
- self-reported trust rather than long-term behavior, and
- limited generalizability to other AI systems, tasks, or contexts.

## Survey vs. Experiment: What Is the Main Difference?

| Survey Study | Experimental Study |
|---|---|
| Measures variables as they naturally occur | Manipulates at least one independent variable |
| Often examines relationships or associations | Often examines causal effects |
| Participants may all complete the same measures | Participants may be assigned to different conditions |
| Example question: "Is trust in AI associated with willingness to use it?" | Example question: "Does changing an AI's communication style affect trust?" |
| Usually cannot establish causality on its own | Randomized experiments can provide stronger evidence of causality |

## What Your Own Paper Should Demonstrate

Regardless of the design you choose, your proposal should make clear:

1. **What question are you asking?**
2. **What does prior research tell us, and what remains unknown?**
3. **Who or what will you study?**
4. **What are your key variables or constructs?**
5. **Exactly how will those variables be measured or manipulated?**
6. **What will participants experience or do?**
7. **Why is this design appropriate for answering your research question?**
8. **What are the major strengths and limitations of the proposed study?**

Your proposal does not need to look exactly like either example. The goal is to demonstrate a logical connection between the **literature, research question, variables, measurement, and research design**.
